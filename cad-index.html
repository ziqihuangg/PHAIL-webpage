CAD Index

Ranks count every fitted model, so a filtered view keeps the original numbers. Each model's best entry on each board is used - usually its best agent harness and effort level.

Index method

Why
No single CAD board ranks the field, and each board alone is noisy - see Board agreement.
Data
Published results from 5 CAD leaderboards whose operators run every model themselves, on one protocol - 0 results for 0 models, copied unchanged from the Ledger. We measure nothing ourselves; numbers the model vendors report on their own set-up are off by default.
Method
The same as the Robotics Index: inside each board every pair of models is compared against the board's noise, boards are weighted by the room they leave at the top, and all comparisons are pooled into one strength per model.
Meaning
A model's index is its average chance of beating each of the other ranked models, taken one at a time, according to the pooled comparisons of all boards. 50 is the middle of this field. It is not a share of tasks solved, and not the share of models it is ranked above.

What is specific to CAD

  1. One entry per modelA board may list a model several times, with different agent harnesses (Claude Code, Codex, mini-swe-agent...) or effort levels. Its best entry counts; the harness is kept in the Ledger.
  2. Noise from the board where it is publishedParametric CAD Bench gives a 95% interval for every run; the index uses it directly (half-width / 1.96). Elsewhere the noise comes from the number of tasks, with a conservative stand-in where a board does not state it.
  3. Vendor numbers are a different claimBenchCAD also lists numbers the model makers report from their own set-ups and subsets. They enter only with Evidence = "+ paper tables", at half weight.
  4. Like-for-like onlyMesh-to-CAD specialists and a board whose models appear nowhere else stay in the Ledger only; the reasons are under Boards and weights.
Details: the boards and their weights, in numbers

The step-by-step derivation with formulas - noise, difficulty weights, Bradley-Terry, the expected-win-rate index and the drop-one checks - is on the Robotics Index. The numbers below are this index's own, for the default settings.

Boards and weights

  • Weight = difficulty × family share × evidence.
  • Held-out accuracy: fit without the board, then check its clearly separated pairs. 50% is a coin flip; red is below 60%.

Capabilities, decoupled

  • Each column is its own small index: the same method (steps 2-7) run only on the sub-scores that measure that capability. A cell is the model's chance of beating the other models on that capability (0-100), not a raw score; · means the model has no such score.
Details: who defines each capability, and the data behind each column

Model gaps

  • Where models on the CAD boards still fall short, read off the raw published scores, not the index.
  • Counted from the Ledger each time the page loads.

Board agreement

  • In short: when two boards test the same models, do they put them in the same order? Kendall's τ scores that from +1 (same order) through 0 to −1 (reversed), on the models both share (four or more). Formulas are on the Robotics Index.

Pooled index against any single board

  • Leave one board out, fit the index on the others, and check the left-out board's clearly separated pairs: how many can it judge, and how many does it order correctly?
  • Compared with the single other board that shares the most models with it.

Index against cost

  • What a result costs to get: Parametric CAD Bench publishes the cost of every run.
  • CAD Arena also publishes a cost per trial, on its own tasks; see the Ledger.
