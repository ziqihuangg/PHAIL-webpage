CAD Index

Ranks count every fitted model, so a filtered view keeps the original numbers. Each model's best entry on each board is used, among the harnesses the board itself runs. PRODUCT marks a CAD product that runs its own harness.

Index method

Why
No single CAD board ranks the field, and each board alone is noisy - see Board agreement.
Data
Published results from CAD leaderboards that grade every entry themselves on one protocol - results for models, read from the boards' own files. We measure nothing ourselves; numbers the model vendors report on their own set-up, and submissions no one validated, are off by default.
Method
The same as the Robotics Index: inside each board every pair of models is compared against the board's noise, boards are weighted by the room they leave at the top, and all comparisons are pooled into one strength per model.
Meaning
A model's index is its average chance of beating each of the other ranked models, taken one at a time, according to the pooled comparisons of all boards. 50 is the middle of this field. It is not a share of tasks solved, and not the share of models it is ranked above.

What is specific to CAD

  1. Only boards that overlapA board joins the index only if enough of its models are also on the others to place the rest. Boards that fail this stay beside the index - in the Ledger, in the Capabilities columns and under Boards and weights, each with its reason.
  2. One entry per model, in the board's own harnessA board may list a model several times, with different agent harnesses (Claude Code, Codex, mini-swe-agent...) or effort levels; the best entry the board ran itself counts. On CADGenBench, where submitters run their own systems, only the board's baseline harness counts; their own harnesses stay in the Ledger.
  3. Products rank, but do not set difficultyCAD products that do not name their model (Godela, Archie in Forge) are ranked as entries of their own. They run their own harness, so a board's difficulty is read from its best baseline entry, not from them.
  4. Run mode is shown, not adjusted forBenchCAD scores one program written in one shot; the other boards let the model work in a tool loop. Each board's mode is listed under Boards and weights.
  5. Noise from the board where it is publishedParametric CAD Bench and CAD Arena give a 95% interval for every entry; the index uses it directly (half-width / 1.96). Elsewhere the noise comes from the number of tasks, with a conservative stand-in where a board does not state it.
  6. Other evidence is a different claimNumbers model makers report from their own set-ups (BenchCAD) and unvalidated CADGenBench submissions enter only with Evidence = "+ paper tables", at weight.

Boards and weights

  • Weight = difficulty × family share × evidence.
  • Held-out accuracy: fit without the board, then check its clearly separated pairs. 50% is a coin flip; red is below .

Capabilities, decoupled

  • Each column is its own small index: the same method (steps 2-7) run only on the sub-scores that measure that capability. A cell is the model's chance of beating the other models on that capability (0-100), not a raw score; · means the model has no such score.
Details: who defines each capability, and the data behind each column

Model gaps

  • Where models on the CAD boards still fall short, read off the raw published scores, not the index.
  • Counted from the Ledger each time the page loads.

Board agreement

  • In short: when two boards test the same models, do they put them in the same order? Kendall's τ scores that from +1 (same order) through 0 to −1 (reversed), on the models both share ( or more). Formulas are on the Robotics Index.

Pooled index against any single board

  • Leave one board out, fit the index on the others, and check the left-out board's clearly separated pairs: how many can it judge, and how many does it order correctly?
  • Compared with the single other board that shares the most models with it.

Index against cost

  • What a result costs to get: Parametric CAD Bench publishes the cost of every run.
  • CAD Arena also publishes a cost per trial, on its own tasks; see the Ledger.
