Physical AI Ledger

Charts / one board at a time

What the boards actually say.

Each chart below plots exactly one published table. Columns are coloured by organisation, so every model out of one lab reads as one group; the badge under each column names the lab and the hover panel carries the full record, including who produced the number. There is no composite index on this page, because there is no shared harness to build one from yet.

Source classes - shown in every hover panel, and on the fill of chart 04

    Real-robot success Robustness gap Capability dimensions LIBERO, by reporter Speed vs success Human preference The same model, two boards Distance to human

    01 / Real robots

    Success rate on real hardware, RoboChallenge Table30

    The clearest real-robot board we track: RoboChallenge runs every rollout on its own machines, so the numbers are not self-reported. The checkpoints, though, are submitted by outside teams.

    02 / Robustness

    Clean scenes vs domain-randomised scenes, RoboTwin 2.0

    Two bars per policy, same tasks, same robot. The upper bar is the clean evaluation and the lower one is the randomised evaluation. The distance between them is the part of a headline number that does not survive contact with a changed scene.

    02b / Robustness, plotted

    Every point below the diagonal loses something to randomisation

    The same RoboTwin 2.0 data as a scatter. A policy on the dashed line is as good under randomisation as on clean scenes. Nothing is on the line.

    03 / Capability breakdown

    RoboDojo simulation scores, split by capability

    RoboDojo scores five things separately instead of collapsing them. Open-vocabulary instruction following is where nearly every policy is close to zero, and it is the column a single average would hide.

    04 / Who is doing the reporting

    LIBERO average success rate, grouped by who published it

    LIBERO is the most-reported benchmark in this ledger and nobody independently operates it - every number is published by a research group, about their own model or about a baseline they ran. The colour tells you which.

    05 / Speed vs success

    Action-generation throughput against task success

    The physical-AI version of a speed-vs-quality plot. It only works here because all three points come from one table, one harness, and one task suite. We do not have the data to draw this across models yet.

    06 / Human preference

    RoboArena pairwise-preference score

    No task list, no success rate - evaluators at eight institutions pick their own task and say which of two blinded policies did better. Whiskers are the reported standard deviation, and they overlap for most of the field.

    07 / Why there is no index

    The same model, read off two different boards

    This is the chart that explains why the rest of this site refuses to compute a composite score. Each line joins one model's number on one board to its number on another. Both ends are cited. Neither is wrong.

    08 / Distance to human

    RoboDojo real-robot track, against the teleoperation reference

    RoboDojo reports a human teleoperator on the same tasks, same robots, same protocol - and the human clears every task. That reference is the only thing on this page that makes an absolute claim about how far along the field is.
